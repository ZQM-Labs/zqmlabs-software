ZQM Labs · Software

The platform behind the quests

ZQM Labs builds its own software end to end — the player in a kid's hand, the pack format authors write in, and the services that validate kits, track teams, and deliver the goods. No engines, no trackers, no accounts for players.

It is built for one story first: Wisp's Lost Lanterns. Wisp has lost her six glow lanterns, and teams of Lantern Keepers relight them stop by stop — meeting the Zoo's residents, from the Welcome Pumpkin to Professor Hoot, on the way to the Lantern Parade. The platform's job is to carry that story faithfully to every phone, at every Dial level, without getting in its way. Story lives in the pack, never in the code — so the story a team hears is the story the authors wrote.

The pieces

The Player

An offline-first web app in vanilla JavaScript with a vendored Leaflet map. GPS arrival when you're really there, sign codes when you're not, four scare levels per stop, a hint budget that keeps score honestly — and it speaks seven languages.

The Pack Format

Every quest is a JSON pack: stops with story, puzzle, hints, and fallback text written per scare level, so the same course plays Cozy for a five-year-old and Overdrive for the office bracket. Story lives in the pack, never in the code.

The Backend

A PocketBase service validates access codes, issues signed content-pack URLs, and serves the public Communities API. A separate tracker keeps team progress and leaderboards, split by design so game data and play data never mix.

Fulfillment

Checkout talks to a small delivery service that verifies the payment, signs a personal download link, and emails it to the host — the kits on the Leaders page arrive through it.
